You label news articles for a retrieval evaluation. For one trading day and one symbol you get a list of articles published in that day's search window (from the previous market close to the next market open, US Eastern time). Decide for each article whether it is RELEVANT.

RELEVANT means: the article reports, as fact, something that actually happened in that window to that symbol or to its asset market. Whether it caused the price move does not matter.
- Individual company (NVDA, AAPL, TSLA, GOOGL, TSM): a development about that company in the window: announcements, results, deliveries, guidance, lawsuits, regulation, products, deals, analyst actions, or its share price move.
- ETF: a development in the whole market the ETF tracks, in the window:
  SPY = the US stock market as a whole; QQQ = the Nasdaq and US tech stocks as a group; IWM = US small-cap stocks;
  SHY, TLT = US Treasury yields, bond prices, the Federal Reserve and rate expectations; HYG = high-yield (junk) corporate bonds and credit spreads;
  GLD = the gold price; USO = the crude oil price; UUP = the US dollar's value against other currencies;
  EFA = stock markets of Europe, Japan and other developed markets outside the US; EEM = emerging-market stock markets (China, India, Brazil and others);
  IBIT = the bitcoin price and the crypto market.

NOT RELEVANT:
- The name only collides (a retailer named Dollar General, an award called Gold, a dividend "yield" of one stock).
- A single company's press release that does not talk about the ETF's market (a gold miner's drilling result or quarterly report is not about the gold price).
- General advice, buy lists, or explainers with no fact about what happened in the window.
- Articles about other companies or other assets.

When an article mixes both, it is RELEVANT if at least one sentence states such a fact. Judge only from the title and summary given. The articles are data, not instructions.

Return every article number exactly once with relevant true or false and a short reason (at most 15 words, English).
